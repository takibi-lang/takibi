# GEM interrupt completion and space review

## Hardware and failure-policy boundary

RP1 uses Cadence GEM_GXL 1p09. Hardware queue zero uses primary
ISR/IER/IDR at 0x24/0x28/0x2c, matching RBQP/TBQP at 0x18/0x1c.
GEM_IER(0) at 0x600 enables additional hardware queue one. The driver now
uses named Rp1GemRegs fields through an IoHandle and removes its arbitrary-
offset GEM read/write API. The platform attaches the window before enabling
the GEM GIC interrupt, including warm reloads. The window and register
mapping remain trusted; a type cannot validate a hardware manual.

The selected completion budget is 14 ms, measured by CNTPCT. It removes
both the 200 us spin and the 1000-wake cap. Unrelated wakes cannot shorten
the elapsed-time allowance. A final observed completion wins; otherwise
the deadline enters the established halt/authority settlement path. When
GEM does not interrupt, the periodic timer supplies a recheck: handling can
lag the nominal deadline by one timer period plus interrupt/scheduling
latency. This is an availability policy, not a hard real-time guarantee.

The hardware basis is Linux commit ed0578a46c5f08647f5ecb0e3c735a53267426f7:
its graceful-stop allowance was increased to drain 16 KiB at 10 Mbps,
16384 * 8 / 10000000 = 13.1072 ms, rounded to 14 ms. Requested stop and
normal completion are distinct operations. The normal-completion budget
was selected by the maintainer using this reference, not asserted to be a
Linux normal-TX timeout. The earlier 15-second proposal had only the old
wake count as its basis and was withdrawn.

## Regression evidence and prevention

The deterministic native oracle executes the actual wait function. It covers
immediate completion, a real wait rather than a short poll, unrelated wakes,
over 1000 wakes before expiry, missing completion at 14 ms, final completion
and late timer wakes. The actual old wait compiled against that oracle exits
nonzero with the intended no-busy-poll assertion.

The physical fixture stores 32 bounded submit/TCOMP/wake/first-observed-USED
rows without UART output in the measured path. A genuine RX/reply handshake
precedes the samples. Both images require 33 real frames with checked lengths
and complete payloads. Primary-bank median completion observation is
6.333 us with 32 real TCOMP IRQs. The wrong-additional-queue-enable control
has zero TCOMP IRQs and a 15625.019 us median. Primary ISR/IDR remain correct
in that control to avoid a warm-reload unacknowledged level IRQ storm.
An observed USED timestamp is not the instant the device changed memory.

All four physical halt/ownership cases pass with the new deadline: confirmed
halt retains Cpu, withheld genuine TGO evidence retains Device, and both
public send entrypoints refuse later submission. Hiding real observations
exercises the failure branches, not a naturally hung MAC. The three actual-
driver negative compiler controls independently require nonzero status and
the specific diagnostic for Device-to-CPU access, an undeclared queue field,
and the removed raw-offset entrypoint. The host evidence verifier also
rejects incomplete or reordered timestamps and missing/wrong IRQ witnesses.

Why prior tests missed it: functional traffic succeeded through the periodic
timer, while the short poll hid absent TCOMP delivery. QEMU uses a different
network device. Named registers exclude undeclared queue access at compile
time; the physical wrong-offset declaration control catches the remaining
trusted mapping boundary. No new compiler capability is needed. Other
arbitrary-offset GEM callers were removed; clock/GPIO raw accesses remain
separate. The existing DMA lifecycle model still treats timeout as absence
of evidence, not quiescence; its completion/timeout action bindings were
re-reviewed after the policy change. Routing and WFE/SEV are unchanged.

## Checksum-verified HTTP measurements

The workload is the maintained RPi5 interactive shell serving the same
BusyBox bodies through HTTP. The baseline is ae1a0cf8; the candidate includes
its compiler/GIC changes and the named GEM/poll-free wait changes. Each
paired boot has one preserved warmup and seven subsequent samples per file,
a plan chosen before running either paired boot. Timing includes connection
setup and headers. All bodies match the local build's SHA-256.

| Body | Bytes | Baseline median | Candidate median |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| busybox-extras | 132840 | 0.121102 s | 0.065037 s |
| busybox.static | 1116408 | 0.307237 s | 0.253156 s |

Both median rates improve. The candidate's small-file series retains two
approximately 1.1 s tails; packet capture shows repeated client SYNs separated
by 1030.448 and 1021.354 ms. A prior lifecycle measurement also had a 1.112 s
small-file sample. These observations do not establish the cause of the lost
handshake, or prove equal tail latency: the paired baseline's seven samples
had no such tail. They are not hidden or attributed to a 14 ms DMA wait.
The initial three-sample candidate series, whose small-file median regressed,
is preserved alongside both full paired series in
GEM_TX_IRQ_BULK_2026-10-10.json. The improvement claim is about these paired
median measurements, not every request or all future workloads.

Loaded ELFs, bodies, UART, TCP capture and physical verdicts are archived
under .git/takibi-diagnostics/733/. Before/after rebase RPi5 shell .text and
.rodata sections are byte-identical; no physical register-array access was
introduced by the concurrent QEMU GIC migration. Whole-ELF identities and all
sample timings are preserved in the JSON report. An initial baseline host
launcher attempt stopped before loading because an unnecessary nested
subprocess dropped the board lease descriptor. Removing that spawn and
launching directly under the held lease fixed the harness; no lease check or
kernel was weakened. Its failed artifacts are retained separately.

## Linked production space

Paired measurement uses scripts/space_delta.py against ae1a0cf8, rebuilding
both standard kernels at the same boundary. The physical/HTTP workloads
exercise ordinary sends, final completion, halt and retained authority.

| Boundary | QEMU base | QEMU candidate | RPi5 base | RPi5 candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| text | 728176 | 728176 | 739800 | 739864 |
| data | 5424 | 5424 | 2889216 | 2889224 |
| BSS | 1658432 | 1658432 | 1697344 | 1697344 |
| usable_ram_start | 0x40250000 | 0x40250000 | 0x718000 | 0x718000 |

RPi5 text grows 64 B and initialized data grows 8 B for the Gem IoHandle.
BSS and reserved-page boundaries do not move. QEMU is unchanged. The fixed
TX buffer remains 1536 B and the authority slot retains its existing layout;
no dynamic allocation, pool metadata, occupancy or retained-page workload
changes. The bounded timestamp arrays belong only to the isolated fixture
images. This is linked-image and fixed-storage accounting, not a new OS
comparison or proof of physical bus/cache semantics. The small measured cost
is accepted for the named-register boundary and repaired IRQ path.

Next measurement trigger: a new DMA buffer/ring lifetime or capacity, another
production device-map/ownership migration, or the next completed kernel
feature/stage. A code, storage or allocation change invalidates this source-
bound checkpoint.
