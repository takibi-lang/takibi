Syscall symmetry and forced-core state removal: linked-space review
Date: 2026-11-09
Base: 793a1d090b59c47b92d476e440bdb52f6b9e9287
Candidate production source: 0e367aca6ec77b2492abe5732b35c826896067bb

Workload and boundary
---------------------
The maintained boot/rootfs, including the affinity probe's descriptor-error
and procfs checks, with the forced-core syscall policy removed. This review
measures the linked image and static symbols, not peak runtime allocation,
physical cache behavior or a timing improvement. The operation uses existing
fd/socket/procfs lifetimes; it introduces no allocator or retained pool policy.
Normal boot and affinity/GDB regressions remain separate functional checks.

Method
------
Run scripts/space_delta.py against origin/main. It builds both production
kernels in the candidate and a detached temporary baseline checkout. A second
run captured llvm-size -A and llvm-nm -S --size-sort for section/symbol
attribution. Both runs returned the same totals and passed the memory-map
check. No external OS comparison boundary changes in this removal.

Removing the forced-core diagnostic field saves static payload, but alignment
and reservation rounding keep total BSS, image span and allocator boundaries
unchanged. This is not a claim of 512 B less reserved RAM, smaller retained
pools or lower peak workload occupancy. Historical ProcessRecord measurements
remain tied to their old source versions, not silently rewritten here.

Assessment and next trigger
---------------------------
Accept the explained 404 B RPi5 read-only increase for the concrete regression
coverage. The removal also deletes per-call clearing/locking paths; no timing
claim is made from image size or QEMU. Do not weaken descriptor checks,
stack ownership or diagnostics to minimize the image. The next review trigger
is the ExecutionState per_cpu migration, RootHeld lifetime changes, or another
completed scheduler/storage feature (including the sleeping-Mutex foundation).
